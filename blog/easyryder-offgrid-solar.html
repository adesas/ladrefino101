Easy-Ryder: Making the Camper Run on Sunlight

#garage #engineering

The 1978 Toyota Pickup wears an Easy-Ryder camper shell, and after a full remodel it's become the most-used vehicle in the driveway. The remodel itself is straightforward comfort stuff — but the part I actually care about is that the whole thing now runs on sunlight.

The remodel

  • New carpet throughout the shell
  • Fresh sound system (because a pickup is an acoustic cavity)
  • New dinette cushions
  • Shower install with its own hot water heater — yes, in a 1978 truck

The power system

Solar array320 W of panels, roof-mounted on the shell
Battery bank300 Ah LiFePO₄ (≈ 3.9 kWh usable at nominal voltage)
Solar chargingVictron MPPT solar charge controller
Driving chargeRenogy DC-DC charger from the alternator to the bank

Lithium was never really a debate. LiFePO₄ gives you roughly double the usable capacity of an equivalent lead-acid bank, a fraction of the cycle-life anxiety, and — for camping — the ability to run deep without cooking your plates. The DC-DC charger is what makes it feel like a normal car: drive all day, sleep with everything charged, and the solar array tops it off while you're parked in the sun.
